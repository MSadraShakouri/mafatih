# Content and translation policy

This is a standing project rule, not an optional suggestion.

## No AI translation of Arabic text

**Do not use AI to translate the Arabic text itself.** This includes Quran verses, prayers, ziyarat, invocations, Arabic quotations and other Arabic passages. Do not ask an AI to translate a Persian or other-language rendition of devotional Arabic as a workaround. Missing devotional translations must come from an appropriately licensed human translation, with its source and translator recorded, or be left missing until an authorized human translator supplies one.

AI may assist with ordinary **explanatory prose**, such as translating a Persian instruction, introduction or explanation into English. Label that work as AI-assisted, identify the source language and passage, and have a person review it. This exception does not authorize translating an Arabic prayer or Quran quotation that appears in an explanation. Keep such quotations in their original form or use an appropriately licensed existing human translation.

AI may help with software, indexing, finding possible mismatches, organizing text and suggesting review candidates. It may not certify religious accuracy, create devotional translations, grant licenses, or mark a candidate as reviewed.

## Existing and new material

- Imported English text and old provenance descriptions are **claims**, not proof of a translator, rights or whether AI was used. Some material needs provenance review. Do not automatically label the entire historical corpus compliant, and do not silently discard uncertain material.
- Never treat a title, a Persian translation of a prayer, or an English introduction as a translation of the prayer body without checking it.
- For any new English translation or explanation, document the exact source, author/translator, language, method, intended use, permission and reviewer. Keep different sources distinct at paragraph/span level where necessary. Fine-grained provenance fields and a review interface are planned; until then record the evidence explicitly with the content change and in the source/claims records.
- Follow `docs/rights-review.md`: a readable webpage, an attribution claim, or an old Arabic source does not by itself grant redistribution rights for a modern translation.

A program cannot reliably detect whether prose was produced by AI. This policy requires honest provenance and human review; the current validator does **not** automatically prove compliance.
